# COPPER COMET
### An original short film screenplay
**Working title codename:** Copper Comet
**Runtime target:** ~9 minutes
**Genre:** Sci-fi drama

---

**LOGLINE:** A retired satellite-repair technician gets one last radio
transmission from a probe she built thirty years ago — and has to decide
whether to answer it before it burns up in the atmosphere.

---

## FADE IN:

### INT. MARA'S WORKSHOP — NIGHT

A cluttered garage-turned-workshop. Circuit boards, old radios, a workbench
lit by a single hanging bulb. MARA OKAFOR (68, sharp-eyed, oil-stained hands)
solders a wire with practiced calm.

An old CB RADIO on the shelf CRACKLES. Then, faint and rhythmic — a pattern
of tones, not static.

MARA freezes. She knows this pattern. She wrote it.

**MARA**
(to herself)
No. No, you're thirty years late.

She scrambles across the room, yanks a tarp off an old CONSOLE covered in
dust — a homemade satellite tracking rig.

### INT. WORKSHOP — CONTINUOUS

She powers it on. Screens flicker. A blinking dot crawls across a star map.

**MARA**
(typing, muttering)
Come on, Comet. Come on, girl.

The tones resolve into a slow, clean SIGNAL: three long pulses, two short.

Mara's breath catches.

**MARA (CONT'D)**
That's not decay. That's not random.
That's *hello.*

### INT. WORKSHOP — LATER

Mara sits before the console, hands hovering over a hand-built transmitter
key. A photo taped to the console: young Mara, 1996, arms around a small
satellite prototype, grinning with a team long since scattered.

**MARA (CONT'D)**
(quiet)
Thirty years. I told them you'd go
quiet after five.

She looks at the blinking dot. It's dropping altitude on the star map —
atmospheric entry in minutes.

**MARA (CONT'D)**
One more year, girl. That's all I ever
asked you for. And you gave me thirty.

She sets her hand on the key. Hesitates.

**MARA (CONT'D)**
(deciding)
Okay.

She taps out a response — three long, two short, echoing the signal back.

A beat. Static.

Then — the same pattern, returned. Again. And again, faster, like
something *laughing.*

Mara laughs too, wet-eyed.

**MARA (CONT'D)**
Yeah. Hi. I hear you.

### EXT. NIGHT SKY (INSERT)

A streak of light crosses the sky — the probe entering the atmosphere,
burning bright, beautiful, brief.

### INT. WORKSHOP — CONTINUOUS

The signal on the console cuts to static, then silence. Mara doesn't move
for a long moment. Then she reaches over and gently switches the console
off.

**MARA (CONT'D)**
(soft)
Good flight.

She picks up the old photo, looks at it, sets it face-up on the workbench
instead of tucking it away.

She picks up her soldering iron again. Gets back to work. Behind her, the
workshop window shows the last fading trail of light in the sky.

## FADE OUT.

**THE END**

---

## Production notes
- Single location, single actor with voice-only "signal" as scene partner —
  built to be shootable on a micro-budget.
- Key prop: the console should feel handmade, not sleek sci-fi — analog
  warmth over digital gloss.
- Sound design carries the emotional back half: the tone pattern should be
  distinct enough that the audience recognizes its "reply" without
  subtitles.
